ORIGIN FILE // FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY

THE MAN WHO BUILT THE TOWN OVER THE COLD.

Edmund Vale // 1889-1964

Edmund Vale founded Stone Hill Ridge after discovering a naturally heated mineral seam beneath the eastern ridge. Public records describe him as a civic engineer who brought work, power, and a permanent water supply to an isolated valley. Private papers show a different ambition: Vale believed the seam could preserve memory by storing heat, sound, and electrical impulse inside the rock.

His first expedition also uncovered a pale fungal growth threaded through the warm rock. Vale called it Mycotherma memoria and mistook its root network for a natural recording system. He cultivated it beneath the archive, feeding it heat, electrical current, and eventually human tissue. The modern plague began when the dormant colony reached the east relay.

He built the first service tunnels around the seam, then placed the town archive directly above its deepest chamber. The archive was not chosen for convenience. It was designed as a listening station.

Founder’s Private Account

“The ridge does not give warmth. It returns what it has been given. We fed it voices, names, and the movement of the town. One day it will return the pattern.”

Vale’s final biography draft ends three pages before his recorded death. The missing pages were later found in Mercer’s evidence locker, written over with the same spiral mark reported by civilians.

What Actually Happened // Reconstruction

  1. Vale’s experiments connected the municipal clock network, archive relay, reservoir pumps, and tunnel lighting into one repeating electrical circuit.
  2. The circuit stored fragments of voices and movement from the town, creating a physical record beneath the ridge.
  3. When the east relay was restarted during the modern quarantine, the stored pattern began replaying through pipes, walls, radios, and human memory.
  4. The Freezing is the body’s response to that replay: temperature loss, neurological disruption, and the sensation of becoming someone else.
  5. Mercer discovered that the outbreak was not random. He entered the archive to shut down the original circuit and was absorbed into the repeating signal.
  6. The altered records and missing civilians exist because the town is attempting to complete the pattern with enough living witnesses to reproduce its founder’s memory.

BIOHAZARD FILE // SPECIMEN F-01

THE INFECTED HOST THAT FREEZES THE LIVING.

Specimen Description

Specimen F-01 is a formerly human host recovered from the east service tunnel. The body remains upright without muscular support, with gray-blue skin, glasslike frost across the joints, and a network of hair-thin fungal roots visible beneath the skin. The host does not speak normally. It repeats fragments of names, evacuation instructions, and Detective Mercer’s final transmission.

  • Core temperature remains below freezing in a heated containment room
  • Root growth follows nerves, blood vessels, and old electrical conduit
  • Host becomes active when another person answers its voice
  • Contact causes localized freezing before visible fungal growth begins
  • Frosted surfaces retain fingerprints that do not match the host

Transmission Mechanism

The plague is a subterranean mushroom colony, not a conventional airborne infection. Its roots travel through damp soil, pipes, foundation cracks, and electrical channels. An infected host spreads the colony by freezing nearby tissue: the sudden temperature drop ruptures cells, releases microscopic spores, and gives the roots a new path into the victim.

Voice contact appears to accelerate the process. The fungus copies familiar speech from the archive circuit, causing victims to approach the host or answer it. The infected do not hunt randomly; they repeat the pattern Vale designed.

Containment Report // Why The Town Is Cold

  1. Vale’s original fungal colony survived beneath the ridge for decades, dormant inside the heated mineral seam.
  2. The east relay supplied enough current to awaken the roots and carry stored voices through the town’s infrastructure.
  3. The first infected host froze a maintenance worker at the reservoir, creating the first active secondary colony.
  4. Each subsequent host became a mobile root node, linking homes, shelters, tunnels, and the archive into one organism.
  5. Mercer learned that destroying the host without severing the roots would spread the spores farther, which is why he entered the archive alone.
